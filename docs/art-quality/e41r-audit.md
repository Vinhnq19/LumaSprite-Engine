# E4.1R silhouette audit

The independent E4.1 assessment approved engineering, requested art revision, and selected B as the refinement baseline. A and B remain available as controls. Final silhouette approval belongs to the Project Owner.

| Criterion | Root cause in B | E4.1R observation at native scale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Head/body proportions | The cap started at row 1, and the silhouette occupied 59 pixels. | A shorter jacket and revised stance place both new silhouettes at 56 px, with 3 px above the cap. B1 and B2 remain taller than A. |
| Silhouette readability | The lifted far leg and large satchel dominated the outer contour. | B1 reads as planted. B2 has an intentional longer stride. The bag envelope shrinks from 9 × 14 to 7 × 10. |
| 3/4 orientation | Nearly level proximal joints and a single torso region suggested a side view. | Both refinements stagger the shoulders by 3 px and hips by 2 px. A separate flat far panel and far-arm occlusion expose the torso turn. |
| Weight distribution | V1 balanced the torso over the near boot and explicitly required the far boot to be higher. | B1 fits the torso projection within the near sole and both boots meet projected ground. In B2 the torso projection moves ahead of the near sole and remains inside the step. |
| Knee/elbow anatomy | Early checks measured a descending chain and overlap, with limited directional information. | B1 bends the knees forward by different amounts. B2 uses a straightened supporting side and a bent leading knee, with a pitched toe. Wrist and elbow directions follow the line into the palm. |
| Prop grip | B used a solid post from the hand to the lantern. | B1/B2 add a separate handle opening and a thumb volume. The hand covers the top rail and the torso clears the main lantern body. |
| Negative space | The wide boot gap in B was associated with a lifted stance. | B1 retains 9 rows of leg gap with two feet down. B2 opens a longer gap in the step. The lantern body has at least 4 px Manhattan separation from the other part masks before contours. |
| Gameplay scale | B was readable by the chest at 1×, with a tall vertical footprint. | All four samples use the same terrain, props and scene anchor. B1 keeps the vertical surveyor profile while grounding both shoes. |
| Overall pose appeal | B's design was promising for the silhouette but its motion intent was unclear. | B1 is the stronger idle candidate. B2 works as a static forward-step reference for later motion work. |

The small opening in the lantern is easiest to inspect at 6×. At 1× both designs still rely on the closed-hand contour and the space around the hanging body for readability. The face and garment remain flat shapes, and the 3/4 read relies on the staggered shoulders, hips and forearm placement. The step checks a single position with a forward weight shift.
